# The future of private browsing...according to Tor users!

## Краткое содержание

В ходе опроса более 50 000 пользователей Tor из 251 страны было установлено, что сервисом пользуются как для повседневной конфиденциальности (избегание слежки провайдеров, защита от монетизации данных), так и в условиях серьёзного риска (обход цензуры, угроза криминализации или насилия). Почти половина респондентов хотели скрыть свою активность от провайдеров, а более чем каждый двадцатый использует Tor из-за угрозы преследования. Важно, что почти 78% новых пользователей впервые знакомятся с Tor через Android, что делает мобильную версию ключевым каналом привлечения. Главными проблемами остаются поломка сайтов (47%) и низкая скорость (42%), причём значительная часть неудобств исходит не от самого Tor, а от блокировок, CAPTCHA и банов на сторонних платформах. Таким образом, выбор приватности всё чаще встречает сопротивление со стороны самого интернета, который делает приватное использование unnecessarily сложным. Результаты опроса дают ценную базу для развития Tor Browser и понимания, каким должен быть приватный доступ к сети.

## Полная статья

Who uses Tor? Public discussions about Tor users often reduce them to a handful of familiar or even harmful stereotypes. To answer that question once and for all, we invited Tor users worldwide to tell us why and how they use Tor Browser, the problems they encounter, and what would make their experience better. More than 50,000 people completed the survey, with respondents reporting Tor use across at least 251 countries and regions.

**SPOILER ALERT:**People turn to Tor both for everyday privacy and moments of serious risk. For one person, Tor Browser offers greater control over who can monitor and profit from their online activity. For another, it's the only way to access news, messaging services, social media, or other information their government has tried to place out of reach. The survey shows how easily the line between “everyday privacy” and high-risk access can shift depending on where someone lives, what they do, or what changes around them.

## Tor is for everyday browsing

![Bar chart of browser usage motivations by categories](/news/blogtorprojectorg/article-ef5d71ba9bdc0c21/image-01.png)

Avoiding surveillance by internet service providers was the most commonly selected motivation, cited by 53% of respondents. Half wanted to prevent the monetization of their online activity. Ideological reasons followed at 48%, alongside avoiding government surveillance at 47% and circumventing government censorship at 44%.

For some, the stakes were higher still. More than one in five respondents said they use Tor Browser because their online activity could expose them to criminalization or violence.

![User testimonials from all over the world](/news/blogtorprojectorg/article-ef5d71ba9bdc0c21/image-02.png)

Respondents’ browsing habits reflected that same breadth. They reported using Tor Browser to access onion services, stream video, read the news, use social media, share files, check email, and publish content. The picture that emerges is clear: More often than not, people need Tor for everyday browsing. As it becomes harder for people globally to browse the internet privately, Tor is increasingly the tool of choice.

![Respondent activism bar chart by category](/news/blogtorprojectorg/article-ef5d71ba9bdc0c21/image-03.png)

## The 'new’ Tor Browser user is mobile

The survey also revealed something important about how people are encountering Tor for the first time. Android users represented 44% of respondents overall. Among new users, that number shot to 78%.

In other words, while legacy Tor Browser users still lean toward desktop, nearly four out of five new respondents were beginning their experience on Android. They were also more likely to browse in shorter bursts and to encounter frustrations related to remembering addresses and passwords, remaining logged in, and navigating the web across sessions.

This does not tell us that new users expect Tor Browser to behave exactly like a conventional browser. Some familiar browser features come with meaningful privacy trade-offs. But it does tell us that new users are arriving with a different set of habits and expectations, and that their first impression of Tor is increasingly being formed on a mobile device.

![Bar chart of Tor Browser mobile users split into new vs. occasional vs. daily users](/news/blogtorprojectorg/article-ef5d71ba9bdc0c21/image-04.png)

And the finding makes sense. If you receive a private message on Signal with a link, are you going to open it on a non-privacy-preserving browser? But that also means, this new group of users is bringing familiar expectations of how a mobile browser should work. Rather than just being another mode to use Tor Browser, Tor Browser for Android is one of the most important places for us to understand what helps someone move from discovering Tor to using it confidently and regularly.

## Choosing privacy should not make the web harder to use

Website breakage was the most frequently reported pain point, selected by 47% of respondents. Speed followed closely at 42%. These remained the leading problems regardless of whether respondents used Android or Desktop, whether they were new or daily users, or whether they were using Tor in a censorship context or because of the threat of criminalization or violence.

![Pain points in aggregate bar chart](/news/blogtorprojectorg/article-ef5d71ba9bdc0c21/image-05.png)

When we looked more closely at website breakage, much of the frustration came from the surrounding web. Among respondents who experienced it, 57% said websites blocked them or that social media platforms banned them. 51% percent reported encountering too many CAPTCHAs.

These systems may be designed to prevent abuse, but their effect is often that makes the web less accessible to people who choose to protect their privacy. Users can find themselves repeatedly challenged, denied access, or presented with websites that simply fail to accommodate private browsing.

Not every source of frustration is something Tor developers can resolve directly. But understanding where the problem originates helps. It helps us distinguish between performance and compatibility issues within Tor Browser, barriers imposed by websites and automated systems, and moments where clearer guidance could help users recover when something goes wrong.

Above all, it shows that the primary obstacle is not a lack of interest in private browsing. People are already trying to use Tor for the full range of activities that make up modern online life. Too often, the web makes that choice unnecessarily cumbersome, and penalizes those seeking more privacy online.

## Takeaways

The findings point to where we need to look next. New users are disproportionately arriving through Android. And they are encountering an internet that still too often blocks, challenges, or fails to accommodate people who choose privacy.

Like any voluntary survey, these results reflect the people who chose to respond rather than every person who uses Tor. But the scale of the response and the consistency of the findings across different groups give us a valuable new baseline for understanding Tor Browser users and improving their experience.

Thank you to everyone who took the time to participate. Your responses are helping us understand not only where Tor Browser needs to go next, but what private access to the internet needs to look like.
